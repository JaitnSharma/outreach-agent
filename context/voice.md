# Voice — how Brace outreach sounds

Match this register when writing the hook or the F2 paragraph. Examples first;
the rules at the bottom are a summary of what the examples already show.

---

## Sample 1 — funding signal, small company

> Saw the $4M seed close last month. Congrats. Usually what follows a raise like
> that is twelve new people and a card that three of them share, and whoever owns
> finance starts spending their Fridays on receipts.

Why this works: it names a specific, checkable fact (the raise, the amount, the
timing), then makes one inference about what that means for *them*. It does not
say "I saw you raised, we sell cards."

---

## Sample 2 — hiring signal, scaled company

> You have four ops roles open right now, including a Finance Manager. Hiring
> that role usually means the spreadsheet stopped scaling about two months ago,
> not that day.

Why this works: the observation is specific and slightly opinionated. It shows a
read of the situation rather than a restatement of a job board.

---

## Sample 3 — competitor already in use

> Noticed you are already on Ramp, so you have clearly solved the card part. The
> thing I would ask is whether the India entity reconciles as cleanly as the US
> one, because that is usually where it breaks.

Why this works: acknowledges reality first, does not pretend to be a new idea,
and narrows to one specific angle where we might genuinely differ.

---

## Sample 4 — F2 middle paragraph

> My case in one line: you raised in March, headcount roughly doubled since, and
> nobody has been hired to own finance yet. That gap is the entire reason Brace
> exists.

Why this works: compresses the whole argument into two sentences and stops.

---

## Register summary

- **Short.** Cold emails run four sentences or fewer before the ask. If it needs
  a scroll, it is too long.
- **Specific over clever.** A real date, number, role or quote beats a good line.
- **One inference, not three.** Say what the signal means, once. Do not stack
  claims.
- **Peer tone.** Writing to a founder or finance lead as someone who understands
  their week, not as a vendor performing enthusiasm.
- **No hype.** No "revolutionary", "game-changing", "excited to connect",
  "hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out".
- **Never fake familiarity.** No "I have been following your journey". We found
  a fact ten minutes ago and should sound like it.
- **Easy exit.** Every email makes it costless to say no. That is what keeps the
  reply rate honest and the domain clean.
- **No em-dashes.** Use a full stop or a comma.
