# Brace — company context

This is the agent's standing knowledge of who we are and who we sell to. Every
prospecting subagent reads this file cold, before it researches anything.

## What Brace is

Corporate cards plus automated expense management, built for funded startups.

Employees get physical and virtual company cards. Every transaction is captured,
categorised and matched to a receipt automatically, so nobody files an expense
report and finance does not spend the last week of the month reconciling a
spreadsheet.

One line: **company cards where the expenses categorise themselves.**

## Who we sell to (ICP)

| Dimension | Target |
|---|---|
| Stage | Raised a seed or Series A/B in roughly the last 18 months |
| Headcount | 20 to 200 |
| Type | Tech / product companies (SaaS, fintech, marketplaces, AI, D2C with a tech core) |
| Finance maturity | No formal finance function yet, or exactly one person owning it |
| Geography | No restriction. India and SEA convert best; US/EU are fine. |

**Why that band.** Under 20 people there is not enough spend to feel the pain.
Over 200 they usually already bought something, and the deal goes to a different
motion. The sweet spot is a company that just got money and just started
spending it faster than one person can track.

## Who to contact

**Small (roughly under 50 people)** — finance is not a department yet, so the
decision sits with the founder or ops lead:
1. Founder / CEO / Co-founder
2. COO or Head of Operations
3. Whoever holds "Finance" in any form (often a Finance Manager, sometimes an EA)

**Scaled (roughly 50 to 200)** — a real finance owner exists, go to them, not
the CEO:
1. Head of Finance / Finance Director / VP Finance
2. Financial Controller
3. Head of Operations / Business Ops lead
4. Chief of Staff (frequently owns internal tooling decisions at this size)

**Never contact:** individual accountants, AP/AR clerks, bookkeepers, junior
analysts. They live inside the problem but cannot buy a tool to fix it. Emailing
them asks someone to escalate on our behalf, which does not happen.

## What we say

Three proof points, used sparingly, never all at once:
- Month-end close typically drops from days to hours.
- No expense reports. The card and the receipt reconcile themselves.
- Live spend limits per person or team, changeable instantly.

## Competitors

Ramp, Brex, Navan, Pleo, Spendesk, Payhawk. Regionally: Razorpay and Karbon in
India, Aspire and Volopay in SEA.

If an account is visibly already on one of these, that is **not** automatically a
disqualification, but the email must acknowledge it rather than pitch as if we
are the first idea they have had. See the edge cases in the skill.

## The four buying signals

These are what the research is actually hunting for. A hook grounded in one of
these lands; a hook grounded in nothing does not.

1. **Recent funding round** — new money means new spend and new headcount. The
   strongest single signal, especially within 6 months of the announcement.
2. **Hiring surge** — several open roles at once, and much stronger if any of
   them are finance, ops, or accounting. Hiring a first finance person is close
   to a buying trigger on its own.
3. **Expansion** — a new office, a new city, a new market. Distributed spend is
   much harder to track manually than spend in one room.
4. **Stated pain** — a founder, ops lead or finance lead publicly saying
   something about expenses, receipts, reimbursements, burn tracking or
   month-end. Rare, and the best hook there is when you find it.

**Signal quality beats signal quantity.** One specific, recent, checkable fact
produces a better email than four vague ones.
